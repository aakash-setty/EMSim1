Learning goals

  1. Spot AF with a fast rate, and the heart failure riding with it.
  2. Look at the ventricle before you choose a rate drug.
  3. No IV diltiazem or verapamil once the ventricle is known to be weak.
  4. Treat the breathing first: positive pressure and a diuretic, no fluid bolus.
  5. Cardiovert for instability, and know why duration matters.
  6. Anticoagulate on a stated risk score, and admit to a unit that can keep going.

The patient

Two problems in one: the rate you see first, and a weak ventricle that decides the treatment.

68-year-old man. AF at 160, saturation 88% on room air, breathing 30 a minute, pressure 132/78. Crackles, raised neck veins, B-lines on ultrasound. Ejection fraction about 30 to 35%.

1. A fast rate starves a weak ventricle

2. Breathing first, and everything at once

  • No fluid bolus. The fast rate is the rhythm, not a dry patient. Fluid goes to the lungs.

3. Look at the ventricle, then pick the drug

  • Diltiazem before the ultrasound is a fair first move. Continuing it after is the error this case teaches. The 2023 US guideline gives it a harm recommendation in a weak ventricle. [UNVERIFIED: class 3 harm per the ACEP Now summary, wording not checked against the guideline text]
  • Judge the patient, not the number. A rate of 108 with a saturation of 86 is not a success.
  • Give magnesium. Cheap, safe, and it helps potassium and rate control. [UNVERIFIED: evidence base is small trials and a meta-analysis, per the case note]

4. Cardioversion

  • Shock for instability caused by the rhythm: shock, ischemic pain, confusion, or heart failure the rate is driving. He is awake at a systolic of 132, so not now.
  • Duration matters. Past 48 hours (US) or 24 hours (ESC 2024), elective cardioversion needs anticoagulation or a TEE first. His AF may be older than he thinks. [UNVERIFIED: thresholds from memory, check both guidelines]
  • If he crashes, shock him anyway: sedate, synchronize, high energy, then anticoagulate.

5. Anticoagulation and where he goes

  • CHA2DS2-VASc of at least 4 (heart failure, hypertension, age 65 to 74, diabetes): anticoagulate. A heparin infusion while he may still need a tube, a DOAC to go home.
  • ICU or CCU, or a step-down unit that can run the mask and intubate in place. Not a ward while he is on a mask.

Traps

  • Treating the rate and missing the heart failure.
  • Continuing diltiazem after the ultrasound.
  • Putting off positive pressure because the rate is louder than the saturation.
  • A fluid bolus into wet lungs.
  • Cardioverting AF of unknown duration without anticoagulation.
  • Calling a rate of 108 a success while the breathing is unchanged.
