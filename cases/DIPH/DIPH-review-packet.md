## 2. The clinical claims most worth arguing with

### 2.0 The seizure is escapable, and this is the largest departure from your case

**What you wrote:** *"A seizure will occur as part of the natural process, regardless of how
well the examinee is doing."*

**What the case now does.** The arrival phase carries an arrow: a patient who has received
**both a benzodiazepine and sodium bicarbonate** moves to the narrowing phase ten seconds
later. A resident who sedates her and treats the conduction before about 230 seconds never
convulses. Your seizure rule is untouched and still carries no guard. It has been given an
escape.

**The guard needs both drugs, and that is what makes it defensible.** An earlier draft
required bicarbonate alone, which was a weak claim dressed as a mechanism: bicarbonate is not
an anticonvulsant. It treats the sodium-channel blockade and corrects the acidaemia, an
indirect argument at best, and the drug that lowered her threshold is still on board and
still absorbing from a gut its own antimuscarinic effect has slowed. **What prevents a
drug-induced seizure is a benzodiazepine.** Requiring both also says something your case
wanted to teach anyway: in the first four minutes what matters is sedating the agitation,
which is generating the heat and the acid and is itself worsening the cardiotoxicity, and
treating the conduction. Neither alone is enough, and neither alone escapes.

**What is still a teaching choice rather than physiology** is that the pair *prevents* the
seizure rather than making it less likely. A patient who has swallowed a gram of
diphenhydramine may seize through an adequate benzodiazepine dose.

**Two consequences.**

*The intended path changed*, from arrival, seizure, post-ictal, narrowing, stabilised to
arrival, narrowing, stabilised. Your seizure teaching is about a third of the clinical
content of this case and a strong resident may never meet it. When you read a debrief, a
resident who seized has not necessarily done anything wrong.

*There is a ten-second dead zone.* The escape needs its ten seconds to mature before the
240-second deadline, so the boundary is 230 seconds and not 240. A resident who completes the
pair at 235 seconds convulses anyway, and then comes out of the seizure and the post-ictal
phase on the flags already set. Same resident, ten seconds later, very different debrief. The
scenario file walks both.

**Deleting the arrow restores your sentence exactly** and nothing else in the pack depends on
it. `DIPH-SEED.md` section 9.8 is the full record.

### 2.0a Amiodarone into the wide-complex rhythm arrests her at once

Added on instruction. It is not in your document at all: your debriefing guide says to avoid
class IA and IC agents and to treat the toxicologic mechanism rather than applying a standard
antiarrhythmic to every wide-complex rhythm, which is the principle, but the specific claim
that amiodarone kills this patient is not yours.

**Why a resident gets here is the point.** A wide-complex tachycardia at 180 with a systolic
of 95 is, on a standard algorithm, an amiodarone rhythm. On that algorithm this is a correct
move. What makes it wrong is that the rhythm is not a re-entrant circuit in a structurally
abnormal heart, it is a poisoned sodium channel, and amiodarone blocks sodium channels among
several other things.

**The strength of the claim, and a disagreement inside the case you should settle.** The
evidence against amiodarone here is mechanistic reasoning and toxicology practice rather than
trial data, and it is weaker than the case against procainamide. So the **tag stays
`discouraged`**, which is the honest strength of the evidence, while the **consequence is
death**, which is the teaching that was asked for. Those two disagree. Move one of them:
soften the consequence, or harden the tag.

**Three mechanical notes.** The tag cannot be `harmful`: a harmful tag halts the case into the
shared halted phase and bypasses every transition, so she would end on generic bradycardic
peri-arrest numbers rather than in the fast wide rhythm this case authors, and the arrest
arrow would never fire. The cost is that the action takes one point off its tab rather than
zeroing it, though the run is still recorded as failed. The arrest phase is now reachable two
ways, so its reason names the outcome they share and both causes. And the arrow exists only in
the wide-complex phase, so amiodarone anywhere else is discouraged and does nothing, which is
arguable on the same grounds as the physostigmine gap below.

**One gap this and the escape made reachable sooner, and it predates both.** The physostigmine
seizure arrow exists only in the arrival phase. It always did, so giving physostigmine in the
post-ictal, wide-complex or stabilised phases was already scored discouraged with no
consequence. A resident can now leave the arrival phase in under a minute, so the hole opens
earlier: give the pair and then physostigmine a second later and the physostigmine does
nothing, because whichever timed rule matures first wins and a rule that is not yet due is
skipped rather than reserved. Rule ordering does not fix it; giving the arrow to the other
four phases would. That is a decision about the case rather than a defect fix, so it is here
rather than done.

### 2.1 Physostigmine halts the case before the ECG and does not halt it afterwards

Your instruction was that physostigmine is harmful and leads back to a seizure requiring
bicarbonate. **The engine cannot express both halves of that as one tag.** A harmful tag
halts the case immediately into a terminal phase and bypasses every transition rule, so it
cannot lead anywhere.

The instruction is therefore split along the line your own narrative draws:

| when | tag | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| the ECG has not resulted | `harmful` | the case halts, with a reason naming the QRS of 132 ms |
| the ECG has resulted | `discouraged` | the patient seizes ten seconds later, and still needs bicarbonate |

Both routes end with the resident being told physostigmine was the wrong drug and that the
wide QRS was the reason. What differs is the score: the harmful route zeroes the
Interventions tab and ends the run, the discouraged route costs one point and leaves the
case running.

**This is the single decision in the pack most in need of your signature.** If you want
physostigmine to halt the case in every circumstance, that is a one-line change to the tag
rule list in `case_2_actions.py` and the seed should be amended rather than the case
patched.

Note also that the gate is `study ecg_12_lead resulted` rather than `ordered`. A tracing
that has been requested and not yet come back does not protect the resident, which is the
right gate and is worth confirming.

### 2.2 The terminal R wave in aVR is authored present, at 5 mm

Your narrative says the examinee should recognise **the lack of** a significant R wave in
lead aVR. Your own teaching text four pages later says there may be a large terminal R in
aVR, and says physostigmine should be withheld for an R in aVR greater than 3 mm with a
widened QRS. Your debriefing guide lists a terminal R wave in aVR and an increased R to S
ratio among the findings of sodium-channel blockade.

Three of the four say the R wave is present. **The case authors it present.** If the
narrative is right and the finding here is its absence, the whole ECG teaching in this pack
is wrong and every tracing needs rewriting.

### 2.3 The chemistry panel's bicarbonate is corrected from 34 to 13

Your admitting form gives serum CO2 as 34 mEq/L and, on the same page, an arterial
bicarbonate of 13. With your sodium of 135 and chloride of 109 the two give anion gaps of
negative 8 and positive 13 respectively. The case carries 13 and treats the 34 as a
transcription error. If 34 is right, the acidosis in this case is not real and the gas is
the number that needs changing.

### 2.4 The three deterioration deadlines, none of which is yours

| from | after | to | claim being made |
|---|---|---|---|
| `seizing` | 120s with no benzodiazepine | wide-complex tachycardia | two minutes of untreated convulsion in an acidaemic patient with a QRS of 132 ms widens the complex to a ventricular rhythm |
| `post_ictal` | 180s with no bicarbonate | wide-complex tachycardia | three minutes of untreated sodium-channel blockade after a seizure |
| `wide_complex_tachycardia` | 120s with no bicarbonate | cardiac arrest | two minutes of untreated stable VT at 180 with a systolic of 95 |

The **direction** of all three is yours: you write that without bicarbonate the patient
develops stable ventricular tachycardia and then pulseless VT. The **numbers** are not.
The third is the one that decides whether this case can kill the patient, and the
deterioration timeline prints the whole path.

### 2.5 Phenytoin is tagged discouraged where you wrote "avoided"

Authoring section 7.3 says halting a case is the strongest claim the system can make and
should be reserved for genuine lethality. The evidence that phenytoin worsens outcomes in
sodium-channel-blocker poisoning is extrapolated from tricyclic work and animal data rather
than established in humans, so it is tagged discouraged with a note that says so. If you
want it to halt, say so.

### 2.6 Antibiotics and acyclovir are recommended on arrival and discouraged afterwards

Your scenario flow asks for a sepsis bundle at time zero, and your debriefing guide never
mentions sepsis. Both are right for the moment they describe, so the tags turn: recommended
in the arrival phase, discouraged once the urine toxicology screen has resulted. **The
switch point is a drafting judgement.** The condition language cannot see whether the
resident has worked out what is going on, so the case uses the arrival of the first hard
evidence as a proxy, which is not the same thing.

### 2.7 The magnesium of 1.6 is invented

It is not in your document. It was authored low so that a resident who checks it finds
something to act on, and so that the case can teach that QT prolongation and QRS widening
are different problems with different treatments. It is a value invented to make a teaching
point, and you should decide whether you want it.

### 2.8 Succinylcholine is discouraged

Not in your document. She has been agitated and hyperthermic for hours, has convulsed, and
has a CK of 540, and rocuronium is available. The hyperkalaemic response to succinylcholine
is well described in established rhabdomyolysis and is not well quantified in the first
hours of drug-induced muscle injury, so the note says the claim is soft and says why.

### 2.9 One tracing is a picture. The second was tried there and taken out

The arrival twelve-lead and the chest radiograph are shown as pictures with no words at all.
A resident who orders one gets a thumbnail in the chart, opens it, and reads it. Nothing tells
them what it shows. Cardiology will read a tracing aloud if called, and calling cardiology is

**The second tracing was briefly shown in the narrowing phase and has been taken back out.**

and then opened a picture that did not look narrowed. The interface was contradicting itself,

I cannot measure a QRS off a scanned tracing whose calibration and paper speed are not

**The restored text is not your wording and was not recoverable.** The original report string

**Two things the remaining image still costs you.** The arrival ECG used to report a QRS of

## 3. The historian is the mother, and that is a departure from the platform

Your case is explicit that the patient is confused, agitated and combative, that she cannot
give a history, and that the history comes from EMS and from the mother, who is a
confederate in the room.

**The engine has no collateral-historian mechanism.** Its interview is patient-facing and
is gated on the patient's alertness. On Aakash Setty's instruction of 5 September 2026 the
mother answers in the patient's place, with no engine change. Every one of the 41 topics is
her voice, written in the third person about her daughter so that a resident who typed a
question meant for the patient can tell from the first reply who is answering.

Three consequences to look at:

1. **Section 10.4's constraints still hold and are easier to hold.** The mother is a lay
   historian, so lay language is natural, and she cannot report a number nobody has told
   her. She never names a diagnosis. She names Benadryl, and only in answer to a question
   about what was found near the bed, and she says herself that she had not connected it.
2. **The history does not disappear when the patient's alertness drops.** In every other
   pack an intubated or obtunded patient ends the interview. Here it is withdrawn only in
   the phases where the team would genuinely have moved the mother out of the room, which
   are the seizure, the wide-complex phases and the two endings. It is available again in
   the post-ictal and stabilising phases, because she comes back.
3. **There is no intubated phase in this case**, unlike every other pack, partly for this
   reason: an intubated phase at alertness 3 would end the interview, and it is not the
   mother who has been intubated.

The disclosure itself is an ordinary interview topic. If nobody asks, the nurse delivers it
in the post-ictal phase, on the toxicology prompt, which is your five-minute rule compressed
into the phase structure. **Nothing in the management depends on it**: bicarbonate is
indicated by the ECG. That is deliberate and it is the point the case is making.

## 10. Engine and tooling changes this case required

Six. Five were gaps that existed before this case and were invisible until it used a
construct no other pack had used; the sixth is a capability the engine did not have. All six
were verified against the other three packs, which pass unchanged.

1. **`sim_runner.py` could not walk an unguarded time transition.** Both of its transition
   loops skipped any rule with a falsy `when`, which silently made authoring section 5.1's
   third pattern, a scheduled natural history, unsimulable. The engine itself handles it
   correctly; only the scenario runner did not. Your four-minute seizure is that pattern.
2. **`sim_runner.py` read neither the catalog's default prerequisites nor its default
   flags.** A study predicate was therefore permanently false and an action whose only
   prerequisite was a catalog default was never blocked, both of which flatter a case. It
   now merges both, and models a study's turnaround so that `ordered` and `resulted` differ,
   which this case's physostigmine tag depends on.
3. **`engine-tests.js` reported a false fairness failure** for a guarded deterioration in a
   phase the do-nothing path never enters, which is this case's post-ictal phase. It now
   says so rather than failing.
4. **`engine-tests.js` measured a vital effect's guard instead of the effect**, by reading
   at a fixed three seconds and by picking a guarded effect. It now prefers an unguarded one
   and reads after the onset.
5. **`expand_interview_variants.py` wrote its expansion into the case JSON** for every pack
   except AFRVR, which meant a pack built by a script silently lost it on the next build.
   The test is now whether the pack has a `case_4_interview.py`, not which pack it is.
6. **The engine could not show a picture.** Every result payload was structured text, so a
   case whose pivot is a tracing could describe the tracing and not show it. There is now an
   `image` payload kind: the pack's `media/` directory is inlined into the build as data
   URIs, a result renders as a thumbnail in the chart, and clicking it opens a viewer that
   closes on the cross, on a click outside it, or on Escape. The validator rejects an image
   that names no file, an image whose file is not in the pack, and an image that also
   carries prose, and warns when it has no caption; six negative tests cover those, and a
   seventh asserts a correctly authored image draws nothing. The other three packs carry no
   media and are unaffected. **The directory is inlined whole**, so a file in it that no
   payload names costs build size for nothing: that is why the second tracing sits in
   `cases/DIPH/assets/` rather than in `media/`, and why one of this pack's own assertions
   is that nothing shipped goes unused.

One defect in this case was found by none of the tooling and only by a case assertion: a
condition of the form `A OR B OR C AND NOT D` parses as `A OR B OR (C AND NOT D)`, because
AND binds tighter than OR, so a non-rebreather mask went on adding five points of saturation
to an intubated patient. It validated, it passed every scenario, and it was wrong. Anywhere
a multi-term OR is ANDed with something, group it.
